### Process Handbook: Comparing the Lenses of the Early Universe

#### 1\. Introduction: The Bounded Observer and the Reconstruction Horizon

In the study of Apparent-Origin Cosmology (AOC), we transition from treating the Big Bang as an undisputed ontological beginning to treating it as a  **reconstruction horizon** . For the advanced student, this distinction is not merely semantic; it is a fundamental epistemological boundary. A reconstruction horizon is the deepest coherent surface from which a bounded observer can retrieve information. Beyond this limit, signal coherence dissolves into cacophony.**Boundary of reconstruction, not beginning of being.**The Big Bang, in this framework, represents the limit of information retrieval for our current class of observables. It is a boundary of our ability to reconstruct the past, rather than a definitive physical edge to existence itself.\!IMPORTANT  **Note to Students**  Apparent-Origin Cosmology (AOC) is a rigorous research program designed to probe the limits of the observer-apparatus. It is not a replacement for the standard cosmological model (LambdaCDM), nor does it claim the early hot, dense universe is "fake." It is a formal framework for testing whether the patterns we observe are properties of the universe or artifacts of the tools used to see it.To understand this boundary, we must first look at the specialized tools scientists use to reconstruct the earliest images of our sky.
