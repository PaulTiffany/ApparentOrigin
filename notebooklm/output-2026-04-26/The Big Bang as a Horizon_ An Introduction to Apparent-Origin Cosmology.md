### The Big Bang as a Horizon: An Introduction to Apparent-Origin Cosmology

##### 1\. The "False Bottom" Metaphor: Why a Floor Isn't Always the Ground

When we look back at the history of our universe, we eventually encounter a conceptual wall: the Big Bang. For decades, standard pedagogy has treated this wall as the "floor" of reality—the definitive, ontological moment where existence began. However, in the research program of  **Apparent-Origin Cosmology (AOC)** , we investigate a different possibility. What if that floor is not the actual ground, but a "False Bottom"? The concept of an  **Apparent-Origin Surface**  suggests that what we perceive as a starting point is a limit of our perspective. Imagine a room where the floor is composed of thick fog. As you look down, you can resolve details to a certain depth, but eventually, the fog becomes so dense that individual objects are no longer distinguishable. To a "Thin Observer"—one with limited resolution—the fog appears to be a solid, impenetrable surface. But beneath that "apparent" floor, the room may continue indefinitely. This distinction is driven by  **Observer Thickness** : the capacity of an agent to preserve and integrate distinctions. A "Thin" observer sees a sharp cliff (the Big Bang), while a "Thick" observer—one with superior tools, memory, or staged computation—can resolve depth further, pushing the "cliff" deeper into the past. **Concept Callout: The False Bottom**  A "False Bottom" is the public metaphor for an  **Apparent-Origin Surface** . It describes a boundary in our cosmological reconstruction that appears to be an "origin" because our access capacity has reached its extremal limit. While our  *reconstruction*  of history stops there, the  *being*  of the universe may not. This visual metaphor of a "floor" connects directly to the formal discipline of the research program: we must learn to distinguish between where our maps end and where reality begins.

##### 2\. Deconstructing the Slogan: Boundary vs. Beginning

To navigate this framework, one must internalize the canonical slogan:  **"Boundary of reconstruction, not beginning of being."**  This sentence establishes a strict division between our epistemic maps and ontological existence. **Reconstruction**  is the process by which a bounded observer uses available data (such as the CMB or redshift) to build a coherent map of the past.  **Being**  refers to the actual, underlying existence of the universe. AOC proposes that the Big Bang is the limit of our map-making resolution, but not necessarily the start of reality itself.

##### 3\. The Observer Quotient: How Resolution Creates Limits

The reason we see "floors" instead of seeing infinitely into the past is that every observer has a finite "thickness." In this context, an observer is any system—a telescope, a computer model, or a human mind—that reconstructs a map of the world. Because these observers have finite constraints, they eventually reach a state of  **indistinguishability** , where they can no longer maintain the distinction between different early-universe states.When details fall below an observer's threshold, they collapse into a single visible boundary. This is known as the  **Observer Quotient**. **Note for the Physicist:**  These constraints induce an  **Equivalence Relation**. Unresolved variation is represented as an equivalence class rather than as further depth; the Big Bang is the "extremal" class where our reconstruction budget is exhausted.

##### 4\. The K Parameter: The Cost of Seeing the Past

In the formal FRW charts of Apparent-Origin Cosmology, the variable  **$K**$  represents the  **Apparatus-Bound Reconstruction Budget** .  $K$  is not an arbitrary number; it is the effective budget of a concrete instrument and inference pipeline, limited by factors like signal-to-noise ratios and angular resolution. In the first FRW chart,  $K$  is explicitly linked to the  **Scale Factor (**  **$a**$  **)** . As we look closer to the Big Bang, the "access cost" to resolve information diverges. The point where we run out of budget defines the  **Reconstruction Floor (**  **$t\_K**$  **)** , governed by the relation  $K = 1/a(t\_K)$ .

###### *Budget ( $K$ ) and the Reconstruction Floor ( $t\_K$ )*

* **Low Budget (**  **$K**$  **):**  Results in a shallow floor; the apparatus resolves very little of the early universe.
* **High Budget (**  **$K**$  **):**  Results in a deeper floor; as we add better tools (increasing observer thickness), the boundary  $t\_K$  moves closer to the mathematical "start."
* **Infinite Budget (**  **$K \\rightarrow \\infty**$  **):**  The theoretical limit where the boundary approaches the "FRW singular boundary." For any finite observer, there is always a  $t\_K > 0$ . The Big Bang is simply the limit where the cost of further reconstruction becomes infinite for our available apparatus.

##### 5\. Scientific Guardrails: What This is NOT

AOC is a disciplined research program, not a rejection of standard physics. It is an interpretive framework that must remain in "controlled, quantitative contact with observation." It adheres to strict  **Forbidden Simplifications**  to ensure it remains a scientific endeavor.

###### *Staying Grounded: AOC’s Relationship with Standard Cosmology*

* x  **Standard observations remain binding.**  Redshift, the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), and expansion are real, observed regularities.
* x  **The Big Bang is not "fake."**  It is a real, measurable "reconstruction horizon."

* x  **Empirical Burden.**  AOC must eventually reproduce or reinterpret results like  **Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN)**  and the CMB anisotropy spectrum without handwaving.

##### 6\. Synthesis: Authorship of Bounds

Understanding Apparent-Origin Cosmology shifts the role of the scientist from a passive recorder to an active agent. Being a "bounded observer" is not a failure; it is the fundamental condition of agency. We gain freedom not by escaping our limits, but through the  **Authorship of Bounds** —the ability to inspect, revise, and "stage" the limits through which we see the world. A bounded observer can encounter an obstruction in two modes:

1. **Exposed Mode:**  The boundary appears as a sharp "cliff" or origin (typical of a single-pass, "thin" observer).
2. **Amortized Mode:**  The cost of the boundary is distributed across memory, tools, and culture, turning the "cliff" into "smooth judgment."
