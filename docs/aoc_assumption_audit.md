# AOC Assumption Audit

Status: canonical guardrail for empirical interpretation.

Purpose:

Make explicit what Apparent-Origin Cosmology can and cannot claim from
observation. This document exists because the first Pantheon+ to DESI exercise
showed that a simple cross-probe distance-deformation question is not obviously
the right question for AOC.

## 1. Direct Proof Is Not the Target

AOC should not be framed as a direct proof of a pre-Big-Bang state.

The core claim is boundary-sensitive:

> The Big Bang boundary may be an apparent-origin surface: the deepest coherent
> reconstruction available to bounded observers, not necessarily the
> ontological beginning of the universe.

If the boundary is a reconstruction horizon, then what lies beyond it is not
directly available in the same observer chart. Therefore the empirical target
cannot be:

```text
observe the pre-boundary state directly
```

The empirical target must be:

```text
detect second-order consequences of treating the boundary as a reconstruction
horizon rather than as an automatic ontological beginning
```

Canonical sentence:

> AOC is testable only through boundary behavior, not through direct inspection
> of what its own model places beyond the observer horizon.

## 2. Zero-Order Control vs. Second-Order Signal

Zero-order control is necessary:

```text
Can the model reproduce or preserve standard observations?
```

But zero-order control is not sufficient:

```text
Can the model produce a controlled second-order deviation, mismatch pattern, or
pipeline-dependent boundary effect that LambdaCDM does not naturally predict?
```

The DESI first pass asked a zero-order portability question:

> Does the Pantheon+ deformation carry directly into BAO distances?

Current answer:

> No, not under the first projection and not at Pantheon amplitude.

This is useful because it rejects a simple shared-distance-shift assumption.
It does not reject the broader boundary-horizon program.

## 3. Assumptions Exposed by the DESI Gate

The first DESI gate assumed:

1. **Shared observable structure.**
   Supernova luminosity distances and BAO ruler distances were treated as if
   they should inherit the same deformation.

2. **Shared amplitude.**
   The Pantheon+ `lambda_K` magnitude was carried into BAO as a candidate
   cross-probe amplitude.

3. **Shared observer frame.**
   The supernova pipeline and BAO pipeline were treated as comparable
   realizations of one effective observer.

4. **Simple distance geometry.**
   The deformation was applied to `D_M(z)` and then projected to `D_H(z)` by
   differentiation, with a second isotropic sensitivity check.

5. **Minimal nuisance structure.**
   A global BAO `alpha` scale was fitted, but the first pass did not yet vary
   `Omega_m`, `r_d`, `w0`, `wa`, or full likelihood machinery.

These assumptions were appropriate for a first control. They are not the whole
theory.

## 4. What Would Count as AOC Evidence?

AOC evidence should look like second-order boundary behavior, not generic
better-fitting.

Candidate evidence classes:

1. **Pipeline-dependent reconstruction floors.**
   Different instruments or inference pipelines imply different effective
   `t_K` values in a way calculable from their specifications.

2. **Stable inter-probe mismatch pattern.**
   Cross-probe disagreements have a repeatable sign, scale, or redshift
   dependence predicted before fitting.

3. **Atlas-coherent boundary effects.**
   Apparent boundary behavior remains coherent under chart changes and is not
   merely atlas fracture.

4. **Horizon-adjacent compression.**
   Near the reconstruction horizon, inferred ages, distances, densities, or
   growth histories exhibit compression patterns not absorbed by standard
   nuisance parameters.

5. **Control-resistant residuals.**
   The residual survives comparison against calibration drift, `H0`, `M_B`,
   `r_d`, `w0/wa`, selection effects, and ordinary astrophysical explanations.

The burden is not merely:

```text
AOC fits better.
```

The burden is:

```text
AOC predicts where reconstruction should fail, how it should fail, and which
controls should not remove the effect.
```

## 5. Gabriel's Horn Model Class

The "cosmological Gabriel's horn" is a candidate model class, not a current
claim.

Informal picture:

> Our Big Bang may be an observer-dependent cross-section or apparent floor
> inside a larger cosmological structure whose deeper extension is compressed
> into an origin-like surface by our reconstruction map.

In this model class, the observed Big Bang is not necessarily the unique
absolute beginning. It is a boundary surface induced by the observer's chart,
similar to seeing a finite opening or floor inside a structure with deeper
asymptotic extension.

This analogy is useful only if it is formalized.

Minimum formal requirements:

1. Define the larger process space `X`.
2. Define the observer chart or quotient map `q_O : X -> R_O`.
3. Define the reconstruction order in `R_O`.
4. Show why a cross-section becomes extremal or origin-like for the observer.
5. Derive at least one second-order observable consequence.

Forbidden shortcut:

> Do not use Gabriel's horn as a visual substitute for a prediction.

Allowed use:

> Use Gabriel's horn as a hypothesis generator for boundary compression,
> observer-dependent cross-sections, and asymptotic depth hidden by finite
> reconstruction.

## 6. Black-Hole / White-Hole Analogy

The useful analogy is methodological, not literal.

For horizons, interiors, and inaccessible regions, physics proceeds by boundary
behavior:

```text
lensing
dynamics
thermodynamics
consistency constraints
stability
failure modes of alternatives
```

AOC should follow the same discipline:

```text
Do not claim direct access beyond the boundary.
Infer only from boundary behavior and controlled second-order effects.
```

## 7. Updated Empirical Question

Old first-pass question:

> Does the Pantheon+ deformation appear as the same distance deformation in
> DESI BAO?

Better AOC question:

> Do different cosmological observation pipelines imply different effective
> reconstruction horizons, and are those differences calculable before seeing
> the residuals?

Most important current task:

> Derive observable-specific AOC maps before fitting. Do not assume shared time,
> shared distance, shared amplitude, or shared observer frame unless the theory
> explicitly earns those identifications.

## 8. Current Program Status

AOC remains:

```text
a formal bounded-observer framework with a plausible cosmological hypothesis
class and a growing empirical burden
```

It is not yet:

```text
a predictive replacement for LambdaCDM
```

The DESI result is currently best read as:

> The simple shared-distance-deformation model is too naive. AOC must either
> derive observable-specific second-order signatures or treat the Pantheon+
> pattern as supernova/pipeline-specific.
