# Differential Geometry Report: The Pantheon+ Atlas Fracture

## Summary
We mapped the **Likelihood Surfaces** for two Pantheon+ atlas blocks (Local
$z \le 0.5$ and Extended $z > 0.5$). The result is an exploratory diagnostic
consistent with an **Atlas Fracture** hypothesis.

## Results
- **Boundary:** $z=0.5$ (The Spectral Plateau).
- **Local Atlas ($z \le 0.5$):** $\Omega_m \approx 0.30, H_0 \approx 73.7$.
- **Extended Atlas ($z > 0.5$):** $\Omega_m \approx 0.23, H_0 \approx 77.3$.
- **Mismatch Vector:** $\Delta H_0 \approx +3.7$ km/s/Mpc, $\Delta \Omega_m \approx -0.07$.

## Scientific Interpretation: Effective Parameter Divergence
The partition diagnostic suggests more than a simple calibration offset
($H_0$): the preferred effective parameters shift when the analysis moves from
the local block to the extended block. This may reflect information geometry,
survey composition, calibration, or model flexibility.

1. **Local Atlas:** Aligns with standard late-universe measurements (high $H_0$, standard $\Omega_m$).
2. **Extended Atlas:** Prefers a lower matter density and a higher expansion
   rate in this partition.
3. **The Stitching Residue:** Hubble-tension-like behavior may include a
   compromise term forced by fitting one model across partitions with different
   effective information structure.

## Grounding the "Science of the Interface"
This is useful because it turns the atlas-fracture idea into a measurable
partition diagnostic rather than only a story about $K$. It suggests that the
**Transition Map** between low-z and high-z blocks may be non-trivial.

The conservative interpretation is that the **atlas-stitching logic** may be
too rigid if it assumes the observer pipeline has the same effective capacity
at all redshifts. AOC provides a candidate framework for modeling this mismatch
as a bounded-observer residue.

**Conclusion:** The Pantheon+ partition diagnostic shows an internal parameter
mismatch worth investigating. It is compatible with a density-catastrophe
interpretation but does not establish that interpretation.
