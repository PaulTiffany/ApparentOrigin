# The Reasonable Scientist's Critique

## Overview
The exploratory diagnostics show notable fit improvements (+18 $\Delta \chi^2$
in one SNIa transition-map diagnostic, +5 $\Delta \chi^2$ in one simplified
BAO transfer check). However, a reasonable scientist must distinguish between
**"A New Law"** and **"A Flexible Correction."**

## Critical Weaknesses

### 1. Overfitting via Step-Function
Applying a shift at $z=0.5$ is technically a step-function correction. While the shift magnitude was measured internally, the choice of a single boundary is a simplification. A more robust model would derive the correction continuously from the **Shannon Density Gradient** $n(z)$.

### 2. Telescope Handover vs. Cosmic Horizon
The $z \approx 0.5$ boundary coincides with the transition between major survey types (e.g., SDSS LRG vs. deeper ELG/QSO samples). 
- **Risk:** We may be measuring the "Atlas Fracture" between different instrument calibrations.
- **Opportunity:** If AOC can quantify the "handover residue" between telescopes, it remains a powerful tool for cosmological metrology.

### 3. The Coupling Constant Problem
We have used $1/p$ as a scaling law for the residue. While motivated by the
Rayleigh dual, we have not formally derived the coupling constant that maps
"Information Volume" to "Expansion Rate ($H_0$)." Without this, the $62\%$
accuracy remains "in the ballpark" but not evidentially decisive.

## The "Anti-Woo" Verdict
AOC is currently a **promising metrological framework**. It identifies candidate
internal inconsistencies in major datasets and provides tools for testing
whether transition-map corrections improve coherence.

Whether it becomes a cosmological program depends on stricter bridge tests,
especially against CMB-linked observables. If the same predeclared mathematics
that improves the $z=0.5$ diagnostics also improves CMB-linked tests without
new tuning, the case becomes materially stronger.

## Strategic Direction
The program should move away from further same-dataset SNIa/BAO sharpening and
focus on the **Information-Geometric Derivation of H0**. The next hard target
is deriving, rather than fitting, the coupling between expansion-rate residue
and observer altitude.
