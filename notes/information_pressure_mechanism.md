# Candidate Mechanism: Logarithmic Information Pressure

## Summary
This note tests a candidate link between **Information Capacity ($\Omega_O$)**
and an effective expansion-rate residue ($H_0$). The hypothesis is that part of
the Hubble-tension-like mismatch can be modeled as an information-pressure term
associated with changes in pipeline information flow.

## The Mechanism: Logarithmic Equilibrium
The working hypothesis treats $H_0$ as an effective conjugate variable to
Shannon Information Flow ($d \log V / dz$). When the observer's flow of bits
drops in the diagnostic, an effective expansion-rate shift may be required to
maintain atlas coherence.

### The Law
$$\frac{\Delta H_0}{H_0} = \frac{\log(\text{Flow}_{local} / \text{Flow}_{saturated})}{\text{Scale Factor}}$$

### Results
- **Log Flow Ratio (Local/Saturated):** $0.8185$.
- **Predicted Tension:** **$3.99$ km/s/Mpc**.
- **Measured Tension (Data Fit):** **$3.67$ km/s/Mpc**.
- **Mechanism Accuracy:** **$91.3\%$**.

## Scientific Interpretation: H0 as Candidate Information Pressure
In this interpretation, low-z and high-z pipelines can return different
effective expansion rates because their information volumes differ. This is a
pipeline/atlas hypothesis, not a claim that the physical universe literally
changes expansion rate because of observer information.
- **Local Atlas:** High density = High bit-flow = High $H_0$ ($73$).
- **Extended Atlas:** Low density = Low bit-flow = Low $H_0$ ($70$).
- **The Tension:** A candidate mismatch term required to stitch across the
  fracture.

## Conclusion: Candidate Interface Law
This is a promising mechanism sketch, not a law. The numerical match is useful,
but the scale factor in the mapping from information flow to $H_0$ must be
derived or predeclared before accuracy claims can carry evidential weight.

**Verdict:** Information-flow gradients are a live candidate mechanism for an
effective expansion-rate residue. This note does not explain the Hubble tension
on its own.
